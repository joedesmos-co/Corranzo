## Latency summary

After the one-time calibration, the live decision uses a rolling 42.7–46.4 ms sample window, an animation-frame hop averaging about 8 ms of scheduling wait, two spectral stability frames (normally no more than about 33 ms), and a three-frame match confirmation (about 50 ms). These overlap rather than add strictly; deterministic corpus measurements must use the attack-to-advance timestamp instead of claiming a fixed sum. Permission prompts, hardware buffers, browser input processing, and background throttling are outside the deterministic budget.

## Register-specific behavior

- MIDI 21–32 is outside the independent autocorrelation tracker's 55 Hz floor, but remains eligible for score-informed Goertzel detection.
- All notes below MIDI 60 receive bass harmonic weighting and boost.
- Weak-fundamental bass may be accepted from a coherent harmonic family, while the musical-shape gate permits the second partial to be strongest only for bass.
- The repeated-note latch has no register branch. Low notes are disadvantaged indirectly: their long decay can keep the gate open and their next hammer attack can make only a modest whole-window RMS rise, so the 1.6× re-arm condition may not be reached.

## First suspected divergence to reproduce

The detector can continue to report the correct low expected pitch on a second attack, but Wait For You can remain blocked at the attack/re-arm latch when the first bass note is still decaying and the second attack raises whole-window filtered RMS by less than 1.6×. This is the first concrete candidate stage; Phase 2 must reproduce it against the actual scorer and matcher before production code changes. Harmonic-confusion and deep-bass detection misses remain separate hypotheses until measured.
